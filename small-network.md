# Lecture 4 — The Small Network

This case did not persist because the relevant people disappeared.

The same patient remained.

The same GP remained from April 2017 onward.

The same neurologist remained.

Later, a movement-disorders nurse entered the same local network.

That is the premise of this lecture.

> **The problem, if established, is continuity without reconstruction.**

The network was small enough that the patient's identity was never lost, the founding neurologist did not become a remote predecessor, and the GP who first sent the integrated problem remained available.

The question is therefore not how a huge system lost track of a stranger.

It is how one authoritative representation remained durable while the same few people repeatedly encountered the same unresolved body.

---

# 1. The record already contains the whole problem in miniature

The sequence is short.

> **GP, 29 June 2017:** “he was attending with bilat foot issues” ... “marked stiffness and increased tone” ... “he claws his toes” ... “?parkinsonian or other underlying diagnosis”.

> **Neurology, July 2017:** “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP.”

> **Patient, immediately before discharge:** “You have not dealt with my feet.”

> **Final synthesis:** “Not clear.”

> **Plan/Recommendations:** “As per above.”

Lecture 4 is about what happened after those sentences entered a small continuous network.

---

# 2. Closure occurred under notice

Before the Protest, compression could still have been an unnoticed feature of ordinary hospital work.

After the Protest, the treating neurologist knew that the patient was identifying unfinished clinical territory while closure was still reversible.

That does not determine what the neurologist was required to conclude.

It changes the information state.

> **Closure under notice is itself a governance event.**

The hospital could have reopened the feet–gait–tone relationship, documented why no further neurological work was required, created a specific follow-up question, or preserved the incompleteness transparently.

The later record must show which of those things happened.

---

# 3. The feet were seen; the relationship is the disputed object

A simplistic allegation that Neurology never saw the feet is unsustainable.

The record contains pes planus, hammertoes, painful foot history and a distal lower limb described in dystonic terms.

The issue is the relationship.

The same lower limb did two evidential jobs.

It helped characterise an unusual young-onset neurological syndrome.

Its painful distal manifestations were also allowed to become “completely separate,” possibly peripheral, and “Not clear.”

Those propositions can coexist.

But coexistence is not the same as independence.

The unresolved question is what work joined or separated:

- pain;
- toe posture;
- gait;
- tone;
- dystonic-looking movement;
- structural loading;
- and later treatment-state behaviour.

That is why the case is better described as:

> **facts preserved, relationships lost.**

---

# 4. The uncertainty was shared; its provenance was not

The same uncertainty meant different things to different people.

The patient had to live with it.

The GP had to practise around it.

The movement-disorders nurse later encountered its consequences.

The neurologist occupied a different knowledge position.

He knew:

- what had been examined;
- what had not been examined;
- that the patient had protested;
- what the final synthesis later said;
- what treatment-state evidence emerged;
- and what he himself continued to write.

So the neurologist did not merely know that the feet were uncertain.

He knew **how that uncertainty had been made**.

That distinction is central:

> **The uncertainty was shared. Its provenance was not.**

For a later reader, “Not clear” can look like residual uncertainty after a completed specialist inquiry.

For the patient, it was an unresolved lived problem.

For the founding neurologist, it sat beside first-hand knowledge that the patient had said the feet had not been dealt with before that uncertainty was formalised.

---

# 5. The neurologist did not inherit his own record

This point should remain exact.

The founding neurologist was not a later clinician reading somebody else's old discharge letter.

He created the neurological synthesis and remained clinically beside it.

The relevant continuities are:

- continuity of knowledge;
- continuity of authorship;
- continuity of clinical involvement.

That makes later persistence different from ordinary downstream inheritance.

When new evidence emerged, the neurologist was in a position to compare it not only with the written record, but with his own memory of how the original formulation had been built.

That is why generic language about “later clinicians inheriting the record” is inadequate here.

The neurologist did not inherit the founding representation.

He stood beside it.

---

# 6. The patient was also a continuous information source

The small network contained another form of continuity:

> **the patient himself.**

The GP carried primary-care continuity.

The neurologist carried specialist knowledge, authorship and provenance.

The patient carried uninterrupted first-person experience of the disease.

That does not make patient interpretation automatically correct.

It makes patient experience a longitudinal evidential resource.

The 2017 synthesis itself described him as having a “very prescient understanding.” The later neurologist response said the letters were intended as a resource for the person himself and to bridge the gap between what clinicians meant and what the patient understood.

The relationship therefore ran both ways.

The patient was not only someone to whom the model was explained.

He was someone whose continuing experience could test whether the model still fitted.

If he repeatedly experienced feet, gait, tension and treatment state as related while the record continued to allocate them to different branches, the proper response was not automatically to prefer one side.

It was to ask whether the parent model still deserved its authority.

---

# 7. Later pharmacology kept sending information back across the boundary

The network did not remain static.

Treatment generated new information.

Professor Healy wrote that the patient “probably has Park II.”

The patient described dramatic motor improvement on procyclidine and rasagiline.

During the planned procyclidine withdrawal he wrote:

> “I am feeling the tension building up, my feet are restless and my walk is falling apart.”

The same neurologist later recorded rapid return of the outside-left-foot phenomenon during withdrawal.

Later records include:

- Parkinsonian interpretation of a foot-drop-like movement;
- OFF/dystonia reasoning;
- toe curling;
- morning foot limitation;
- and dopaminergic effects on pain.

None of those events proves that every painful foot symptom was dystonia.

Together, they repeatedly test the founding boundary.

The longitudinal question is therefore not whether the system ever updated.

It plainly did.

It is whether those updates were local, or whether they caused the parent model to be rebuilt.

> **Local updating, global non-revision.**

That is the pattern to test.

---

# 8. The network was small enough that distance is not a sufficient explanation

Physical proximity proves little by itself.

It does not prove that one clinician read a particular note, made a telephone call, or discussed one event with another.

But it removes a common explanation.

This is not a story of:

- repeated hospital changes;
- unfamiliar GPs;
- geographical fragmentation;
- or anonymous handoffs across decades.

The actors remained comparatively close.

So if the representation remained fragmented, attention shifts toward:

- specialist authority;
- stable problem representation;
- specialty ownership;
- path dependence;
- and the cost of reopening a model that already organises care.

No motive needs to be inferred.

A representation can become durable simply because it works well enough to coordinate action.

---

# 9. Record continuity is not the same as independent revalidation

The record accumulated many pages.

Some contain genuinely new evidence.

Some contain reinterpretation.

Some repeat older language.

Those categories should not be conflated.

A proposition can gain authority because it is repeatedly copied, summarised and relied upon.

That is different from repeated independent re-derivation.

The question is:

> **Did the record accumulate evidence, or did it accumulate authority around an existing representation?**

Sometimes it did both.

That is why the genealogy of important propositions matters.

Possible neuropathy, separation language, dystonia, OFF phenomena and treatment response should each be traced to their source rather than counted by frequency alone.

---

# 10. The same asymmetry becomes much more serious in 2023

By March 2023, the painful distal territory had become a safety problem.

The GP wrote:

> “causing death wish.”

> “I am worried about him.”

> “Despite his facade, I don't think he is doing well.”

> “I'd be grateful for your review before we get into trouble here.”

The movement-disorders nurse reported:

> “no quality of life”.

There was genuine medical work around this period: medication review, neuropathy thinking, OFF/dystonia reasoning, Fabry investigation and neurological reassessment.

The claim is not that nothing happened.

The question is whether the following were assembled into one governing object:

> **pain + mobility + medication burden + falls/balance + psychological endurance.**

The small-network significance is sharper still.

The neurologist receiving this information was not simply encountering severe unexplained foot pain for the first time.

He also possessed the provenance of the founding uncertainty.

That does not turn the Protest into a suicide-risk datum.

It means the same clinician knew both:

1. how severe the present problem had become; and
2. that the same territory had been identified as unfinished before the original uncertainty was formalised.

The question is whether those two pieces of knowledge were joined.

---

# 11. The 2025 complaint identified the effect before the patient had reconstructed the mechanism

The March 2025 complaint alleged:

- diagnostic anchoring;
- failure to reassess;
- fixed framing;
- hierarchical influence;
- and institutional failure of self-correction.

At that point, the patient had not yet reconstructed the Protest as the central founding event in the way the repository now does.

That chronology matters.

The complaint identified the **effect** before the later reconstruction identified a possible **mechanism**.

The Protest did not create the allegation of anchoring.

It later supplied a candidate explanation for how the longitudinal effect may have begun.

---

# 12. The same neurologist then became the principal historical explainer

The treating neurologist's 2025 response is important evidence.

It contains first-hand historical knowledge.

It explains treatment strategy.

It records that Sinemet helped morning pain and that Sinemet CR produced profound relief.

It also says:

> “In essence we have known that Mr Moloney has Parkinsons”.

That response should be read carefully.

It is valuable precisely because it comes from the clinician who remained beside the history.

But it is not independent validation of that history.

The appropriate distinctions are:

- first-hand historical evidence;
- retrospective clinical explanation;
- self-assessment of prior conduct;
- independent re-derivation by another reviewer.

Continuity of actor makes those distinctions more important, not less.

---

# 13. Formal governance arrived later, but the governance problem was older

QPS, PAR and SIMT are later formal structures.

They entered a case in which the relevant governance question already existed:

> **What happens when a patient identifies an omitted clinical territory before closure?**

The later sequence became:

> **clinical closure → longitudinal maintenance → formal challenge → retrospective governance validation**

The SIMT outcome stated that decisions were appropriate and that there was:

> “no deficit in the care provided.”

That conclusion is an authority-producing event.

Its weight depends on what was independently reconstructed.

This gives the case two major objects whose provenance matters:

**Object A — 2017 clinical closure**

What material entered the synthesis? What changed? Who contributed? What happened after the Protest and before finalisation?

**Object B — 2025 governance validation**

What material entered PAR/SIMT? What allegations survived? What was independently reviewed? What came from the founding record or treating neurologist's account?

The point is not that Object B was necessarily a rubber stamp.

The point is that institutional labels do not establish independence by themselves.

---

# 14. Representation can govern the patient's own understanding

An authoritative clinical model does not only organise professionals.

It can organise the patient's understanding of what counts as disease.

If the model says:

- Parkinsonism is central;
- the painful feet may be separate;
- neuropathy may explain the pain;
- Podiatry owns the structural problem;
- and the residual relationship is “Not clear”;

then the patient may spend years trying to understand those experiences as different problems.

That matters because the neurologist later said the letters were designed to help the patient understand “the facts and the nuances” and to bridge the gap between what was said and what was understood.

So the informational asymmetry is deeper than a missing historical fact.

> **The clinician knew how the uncertainty had been made; the patient had to use the uncertainty to make sense of his life.**

That is one reason the later recovery of the Protest and the genotype–phenotype–pharmacology relationship has such force for the patient.

It restores provenance to an uncertainty he had been living inside.

---

# 15. The strongest fair defence

The hospital has serious answers.

The presentation was heterogeneous.

Structural disease was real.

Dystonia was noticed.

Podiatry was appropriate.

Later clinicians did reconsider individual questions.

The 2020 distal reassessment shows the architecture was not frozen.

The 2023 period contains genuine neurological work.

Continuity of one neurologist can support good care as easily as it can support path dependence.

Those points materially weaken any simple theory of neglect or complete non-reassessment.

The remaining proposition is narrower:

> **The record shows repeated management and local reconsideration more clearly than it shows a clean-sheet reconstruction of the original feet–pain–toe-posture–gait–tone relationship by the small network that continuously possessed the patient and the relevant history.**

That proposition is falsifiable.

It would be materially weakened by contemporaneous evidence showing:

- explicit ownership of the unresolved relationship;
- movement-disorders-level phenotyping;
- cross-specialty feedback;
- defined reopening triggers;
- deliberate integration of treatment-state evidence;
- and independent later reconstruction of the founding model.

---

# 16. The mature formulation

The small-network case can be stated calmly.

A high-dimensional clinical problem entered specialist care.

The patient identified an unfinished territory before closure.

The final synthesis converted that territory into legitimate uncertainty and an actionable plan.

The same neurologist remained beside the patient, the formulation and later boundary-crossing evidence.

The same GP remained beside the patient from before the admission onward.

The patient carried uninterrupted embodied continuity.

Later treatment, Podiatry, OFF/dystonia reasoning, pain crises and complaint processes generated real new information.

What remains difficult to locate is the moment when those streams were globally reassembled against the original question.

That is why the central sentence survives:

> **The uncertainty was shared. Its provenance was not.**

And the governing question is:

> **How did the same patient keep bringing the same unresolved lower-limb territory back to the same small clinical network while the one clinician who knew how the founding uncertainty had been created remained beside both the patient and the accumulating evidence, without the relationship being clearly rebuilt from first principles?**

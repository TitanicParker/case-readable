# Adversarial stress-test

The governing question is not whether the hospital can tell a medically plausible story. It is whether, proposition by proposition, the hospital can produce an **evidential bridge** strong enough to answer the serious documentary case. The required sequence is therefore: raw evidence → established fact → documentary inference → serious proposition → hospital answer → evidential support for that answer → what weakens the proposition → what defeats it.

This exercise is bounded by `lesson.md` and the lossless case formulation. No separate bundle of additional primary records accompanied this particular instruction. Where the formulation identifies an unresolved source gap, I therefore preserve it rather than assuming what an unseen primary record would show.

# 1. The strongest fair hospital case

The hospital's best case is substantial.

The patient did not have one simple lower-limb phenomenon. There was genuine structural foot disease. There were corns, callouses, pressure lesions, clawed toes and podiatric abnormalities. The patient himself distinguished some painful foot phenomena from his wider sensations of tension. Severe young-onset Parkinsonism was strongly demonstrated. The dystonic-looking gait was not simply missed: Neurology recorded it. PARK2/PRKN entered the specialist reasoning. External specialist input was obtained. Treatment was active. Later clinicians did not mechanically follow a frozen model: the 2020 foot-throw episode involved specific discrimination, and the 2023 review reconsidered ON/OFF dystonia, timing, neuropathy and other possibilities. The case formulation itself expressly recognises both documentary inheritance and genuine local reassessment.

The hospital can therefore formulate its case as follows:

> The patient's lower-limb presentation contained several genuinely different phenomena. Clinicians reasonably decomposed those phenomena for investigation and management while correctly recognising and treating a strongly supported Parkinsonian disorder. Real structural pathology properly went to Podiatry; peripheral or neuropathic mechanisms were legitimate possibilities; dystonic phenomena were recognised where encountered; genetic uncertainty was retained; and subsequent symptoms were reconsidered as the phenotype evolved. What the claimant calls “path dependence” may instead be ordinary, adaptive chronic-disease care conducted under unavoidable uncertainty.

That is not a straw man. The stress-test instructions themselves require that defence to be taken seriously.

But its **component facts** being true does not establish its ultimate conclusion. The proposition that the original decomposition was clinically justified remains expert-dependent. The proposition that later reassessment was sufficient to validate the parent architecture remains partly documentary and partly expert-dependent.

The hospital has already defeated several overbroad allegations. It has substantial evidence against “the feet were ignored,” “nobody reconsidered,” “there was no structural disease,” “dystonia was never noticed,” and “the system was completely frozen.” The case formulation itself quarantines those propositions.

The remaining case is narrower.

# Proposition 1 — The integrated presentation was divided before the surviving record shows an equivalent bridge for the division

### Raw evidence

The pre-hospital GP object was relational: bilateral feet problems, abnormal gait, increasing tone and stiffness, difficulty initiating movement, inability to relax, toe clawing, deteriorating dexterity and declining function travelled under an open neurological question. Neurology subsequently documented severe Parkinsonian signs and a lower-limb appearance “somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture — NOT FOOT DROP.” The specialist synthesis nevertheless permitted the painful, burning and structural foot material to become potentially “completely separate” or peripheral.

### Established fact

A broad clinical object entered the hospital. Parkinsonism was recognised. A dystonic-looking lower-limb sign was recorded. Genuine structural foot abnormalities existed. A separate/peripheral branch was also created.

### Documentary inference

The Parkinsonian/genetic branch is visibly reasoned at substantially greater resolution than the distal structural–dynamic–pain relationship. The case formulation expressly says the founding separation became more durable than the evidence demonstrating its independence.

### Serious proposition

**A broad unresolved presentation appears to have been decomposed into explanatory branches before the surviving record shows an equivalently detailed evidential bridge demonstrating why those branches should thereafter bear substantial independent longitudinal weight.**

### Strongest hospital answer

The hospital says the phenomena actually *were* heterogeneous. Structural pathology was objectively present. The patient's own history distinguished some sensations. Pain characteristics could rationally suggest a peripheral process. Parkinsonism was independently strong. Dystonia was noticed. More than one mechanism can coexist, and medicine does not require the cause of every symptom to be settled before useful treatment begins.

### Status of that answer

**Partly demonstrated; ultimate adequacy is expert-dependent.**

The factual premises are well supported. The proposition that those facts *clinically justified this particular partition at this particular stage* is not established by the documentary record alone.

### What evidential bridge the hospital still needs

The hospital needs the positive and negative discriminators that earned the partition: distal power, sensory findings, dynamic posture, pain distribution and provocation, fixed-versus-dynamic deformity, loading mechanics, evidence favouring a peripheral mechanism, evidence arguing against neurological contribution, and some explanation of how coexistence was distinguished from independence. Those are precisely the questions the stress-test directs at this proposition.

### What merely weakens the proposition

Evidence that some distal examination occurred; clear phenomenological separation of particular symptoms; convincing structural explanations for particular local pain; and contemporaneous patient statements distinguishing different experiences.

All of those weaken the proposition.

None necessarily destroys it, because the proposition concerns the **relationship among the branches**, not whether individual components existed.

### What defeats it

A contemporaneous 2017 record demonstrating a sufficiently detailed lower-limb reconstruction, explicit mixed-mechanism reasoning, appropriate discriminators and a reasoned explanation for assigning the painful foot territory to a substantially separate branch.

That would be a **documentary defeat**.

A suitably qualified expert concluding that the examination and decomposition were clinically appropriate notwithstanding sparse documentation could produce a **clinical defeat** of a breach theory without necessarily disproving the documentary observation that the bridge is poorly visible.

# Proposition 2 — Facts may have been preserved while the original relationships were weakened

### Raw evidence

The hospital did not simply lose all the GP information. Neurology recovered many component facts. The dystonic-looking gait appears. Structural feet appear. Pain appears. Parkinsonism appears.

The problem identified in the formulation is relational: did the record continue carrying the arrows among feet, gait, tone, posture, pain, loading and movement state? The case expressly distinguishes fact recovery from relationship preservation.

### Established fact

The specialist record contains many of the nouns that had appeared in primary care.

### Documentary inference

The fact that individual elements reappear does not itself show that the original unresolved relationship among those elements remained an active object of investigation.

### Serious proposition

**The documentary system appears better at preserving individual findings than at preserving, governing and adjudicating the unresolved relational question that originally connected them.**

### Strongest hospital answer

The hospital can deny any relational loss. It can say clinicians understood that different mechanisms could coexist, allocated components to the services best suited to treat them, and continued to regard the patient as one person with interacting clinical problems.

That answer is perfectly plausible.

### Status

**Plausible but only partly demonstrated.**

There are cross-domain signals in the record, including later GP reintegration. That undermines any assertion of complete fragmentation. But a contemporaneous explicit governance statement for the original relationship is much less visible.

### Hospital must explain

Where was the relationship itself carried?

Not merely “feet recorded.”

Not merely “Parkinsonism recorded.”

Not merely “pain discussed.”

The stronger defence would show something equivalent to:

*the structural foot problem may interact with Parkinsonian/dystonic posture or gait; that relationship remains unresolved; this service owns X; this other service owns Y; these observations will be fed back; these findings will trigger reconstruction.*

The stress-test correctly makes explicit relational formulation, ownership, cross-specialty feedback and reassessment triggers the decisive counter-evidence.

### What weakens it

Any contemporaneous note expressly linking the branches.

The GP updating Podiatry with Parkinsonian information weakens a simplistic service-silo account.

Later clinicians considering dystonia, pain and medication state together also weakens it.

### What defeats it

A documented relational formulation coupled with clear ownership, a feedback loop and predefined reconsideration architecture.

If the arrows were deliberately preserved and governed, “relationship loss” substantially collapses.

# Proposition 3 — Operational closure may have preceded epistemic closure

### Raw evidence

The 2017 synthesis retained uncertainty around the painful/peripheral branch, yet the pathway became actionable. The record's governing formulation explicitly describes “As per above” as making the mixed information state operational without demonstrating that every relationship had been epistemically closed.

### Established fact

Treatment, referral, rehabilitation and specialist follow-up could proceed while important causal questions remained unresolved.

### Documentary inference

The system had enough certainty to **act**, without necessarily having enough evidence to say that every relationship had been **settled**.

### Serious proposition

**Operational closure occurred around a mixed and partly unresolved information state, but the surviving record less clearly identifies who owned the residual uncertainty and what would cause the parent model to be reopened.**

### Strongest hospital answer

That is normal medicine.

Working diagnoses are normal.

Parallel referrals are normal.

Unresolved questions are normal.

Clinicians can treat likely or tractable mechanisms while retaining uncertainty and revising the model when evidence changes.

That answer is strong.

### Status

**Principle demonstrated; adequacy of the actual reopening mechanism partly demonstrated and expert-dependent.**

The record shows later reassessment, so it cannot fairly be said there was no reopening capacity.

The question is whether that capacity was adequately tied to the **founding relationship**.

### Hospital must explain

Who retained responsibility for the unresolved lower-limb relationship?

What was Podiatry expected to report?

What findings would trigger Neurology to reopen the parent model?

Did the peripheral hypothesis retain its provisional status as it travelled?

What made “not necessary to resolve now” become “necessary to resolve now”?

### What weakens it

Evidence of later local reconsideration.

The 2020 and particularly the 2023 reasoning substantially weaken an assertion of complete operational foreclosure.

### What defeats it

An explicit contemporaneous reopening architecture: provisional classification, named owner, feedback requirements and defined clinical triggers.

That would demonstrate that operational action did not convert uncertainty into ungoverned closure.

# Proposition 4 — Specialist authority may have made the founding representation generative

### Raw evidence

The specialist output carried legitimate authority. It generated treatment, referrals, service destinations, correspondence and terminology. The case formulation expressly warns that authority increases portability but not necessarily evidential independence.

Later records repeatedly use inherited neurological and pain terminology. The patient eventually uses the vocabulary available in his medical environment. The document therefore warns against counting repeated wording as repeated independent confirmation.

### Established fact

Later records are not genealogically independent of earlier specialist records.

### Documentary inference

Some apparent longitudinal corroboration may reflect inheritance from the originating representation rather than independent reconstruction.

### Serious proposition

**The specialist representation became authority-bearing and generative; therefore documentary multiplicity cannot automatically be treated as multiple independent confirmations of the propositions embedded in it.**

### Strongest hospital answer

Later clinicians did not simply copy text.

They repeatedly encountered the patient.

They made new observations.

They performed examinations.

They changed treatment.

The 2020 and 2023 records in particular show genuine fresh discrimination.

So continuity may reflect not mere inheritance, but repeated clinical compatibility with the model.

### Status

**Partly demonstrated.**

This is among the hospital's stronger answers. The formulation itself recognises genuine local reassessment and warns against describing the architecture as frozen.

What remains unproved is that those later independent events independently revalidated the *originating partition* rather than particular downstream propositions.

### Hospital must explain

For every later document relied upon as corroboration:

Was the author testing the inherited proposition?

What source material did they receive?

What new examination was done?

What competing explanations were considered?

What new evidence supported the category?

What could have falsified it?

Was the conclusion genuinely re-derived?

### What weakens it

Any genuinely independent examination supporting the same category.

The 2020 foot-throw discrimination and 2023 reasoning weaken a pure inheritance theory considerably.

### What defeats it

Several genuinely independent first-principles reconstructions in which later clinicians understand the contested relationship, test competing mechanisms and nevertheless re-derive substantially the same separation.

That transforms **continuity** into genuine **convergence**.

# Proposition 5 — Later boundary-crossing evidence repeatedly tested the original partition, but the record shows local updating more clearly than parent-model reconstruction

### Raw evidence

The procyclidine period links neurological treatment state with gait, tension and foot phenomena; withdrawal was followed by deterioration involving the feet and gait, and restoration by improvement. The document expressly refuses to treat this as proof of mechanism.

Later evidence includes cramping, toe curling, Parkinsonian lower-limb classification, OFF phenomena, medication-state variation, a GP report that previous foot/lower-leg pain had improved with better Parkinson's control, and by 2025 a reported profound Sinemet CR response.

### Established fact

Later lower-limb observations did not remain neatly on one side of the original clinical partition.

### Documentary inference

The partition repeatedly received information capable of testing its boundaries.

### Serious proposition

**The later record shows repeated boundary-crossing evidence, yet it shows proposition-specific local adjustment more clearly than a first-principles reconstruction of the founding structural–pain–dystonia–gait model.**

### Strongest hospital answer

Not every new observation should cause an eight-year case to be rebuilt from scratch.

Good chronic-disease medicine may work precisely by answering the question presented at each stage.

A new foot-throw is examined.

OFF dystonia is considered.

Abrupt onset is weighed against peripheral neuropathy.

Fabry is considered.

Medication timing is addressed.

Treatment is adapted.

That could be the appropriate level of clinical response.

### Status

**Substantially supported as a defence against a “frozen model” allegation; whether it defeats the broader reconstruction proposition is expert-dependent.**

The hospital's 2023 evidence is particularly important. The case must concede genuine reasoning.

### Hospital must explain

The answer now has to be event-specific.

Why did the procyclidine withdrawal/rechallenge pattern not require broader reconsideration?

Why did the later Parkinsonian classification of a foot phenomenon not illuminate the earlier lower-limb history?

Why did OFF dystonia not require wider historical reconstruction?

What distinguished the 2023 recurrent pain from the earlier problem?

Why did repeated GP links between pain and Parkinsonian control not require reconstruction?

Why was Sinemet CR response treated as relevant to present treatment without necessarily revisiting classification history?

### What weakens it

Every documented local reassessment.

The 2020 and 2023 material significantly reduce the force of the proposition.

### What defeats it

A pre-complaint specialist synthesis expressly gathering structural pathology, pain, dystonic posture, gait, OFF state, Parkinsonian treatment state, neuropathic possibilities and genetics, and then explaining why the original partition remained the best clinical model.

The stress-test identifies exactly that as the major defence.

# Proposition 6 — PRKN remained genuinely unresolved while later terminology could imply greater settlement

### Raw evidence

PARK2/PRKN was not a hindsight invention. Professor Healy documented that the patient probably had PARK2 and recommended testing; by 2018 the treating record still identified PARK2/PRKN as the suspected cause.

The later record nevertheless contains terminology capable of presenting the Parkinsonian condition more affirmatively, while the formulation says no clear pre-2025 bridge has yet been identified showing explicit PRKN closure.

### Established fact

There is strong continuity of the Parkinsonian syndrome.

There was also a specific unresolved etiological hypothesis.

Those are different propositions.

### Documentary inference

Later syndrome-level language may have become more settled without a correspondingly visible documentary resolution of etiology.

### Serious proposition

**A specific, contemporaneous PRKN/PARK2 hypothesis remained unresolved while later terminology could convey more etiological settlement than the visible closure history warrants.**

### Strongest hospital answer

Clinical diagnosis and molecular etiology are different.

A doctor can confidently diagnose and treat Parkinson's disease while the precise genetic mechanism remains unknown.

Genetic testing may reasonably be deferred if it does not alter current management.

“Idiopathic” or similar terminology may function as practical phenotype shorthand rather than as a formal statement that monogenic disease has been disproved.

That is a powerful answer.

### Status

**Partly demonstrated; semantic and clinical meaning remain expert-dependent.**

The record itself shows sophisticated Parkinsonian reasoning and explicit genetic uncertainty. What remains unclear is the precise bridge between that uncertainty and later terminology.

### Hospital must explain

Was PRKN actually tested earlier?

Was it clinically downgraded?

Was it formally rejected?

Did “idiopathic” mean non-genetic etiology, or was it merely ordinary clinical shorthand?

Did later clinicians know PRKN remained open?

What was supposed to trigger testing?

### What weakens it

Evidence that later language consistently distinguished phenotype from etiology.

Evidence that “idiopathic” was plainly used loosely even while recessive disease remained under discussion.

### What defeats it

A clear PRKN closure bridge: negative testing, documented reasoned downgrading, specialist rejection, or explicit contemporaneous records explaining that the molecular question remained open and that the clinical label did not purport to settle it.

The later 2026 result should not be used to substitute for this missing historical bridge. The document expressly treats the endpoint as confirming that Parkinsonism and dystonia ultimately belonged to PRKN disease while warning that it cannot prove the mechanism of every earlier foot-pain episode.

# Proposition 8 — The complaint response may show continuity more clearly than independent revalidation

### Raw evidence

The complaint response continued to use established pain terminology and accommodated the Sinemet response by explaining that Parkinson's medication could affect pain “of whatever cause.” It also relied on the accumulated letters and earlier clinical history.

The governing formulation expressly distinguishes self-explanation from independent revalidation and asks whether the hospital reconstructed the clinical model or principally evaluated the reasonableness of decisions made inside it.

### Established fact

The 2025 response contains substantial historical explanation.

It is relevant evidence of the originating clinician's rationale.

### Documentary inference

An explanation demonstrating that care was coherent **within** an established model is not automatically proof that the originating model was independently tested.

### Serious proposition

**When the architecture itself was challenged, the later response appears to explain the continuity and reasonableness of management more clearly than it independently reconstructs the provenance and revalidation of the categories under challenge.**

### Strongest hospital answer

That may be exactly what a complaint review was supposed to do.

The hospital can say it reviewed contemporaneous records, considered investigations and treatments, obtained appropriate clinical input and reasonably concluded the care was defensible.

It is not automatically obliged to conduct a new academic reconstruction of every historical category.

### Status

**Partly demonstrated.**

The response plainly contains substantive reasoning.

What is less visible in the supplied architecture is an independent first-principles provenance audit.

### Hospital must explain

What question did the complaint process actually set itself?

Did it ask merely whether historical decisions were reasonable from the recorded information state?

Or did it separately ask whether the disputed information state itself was properly constructed and subsequently revalidated?

Who reviewed the source materials?

Was the original GP object reconstructed?

Were contrary events actively tested?

Was PRKN genealogy examined?

Was independent specialist expertise obtained on the contested architecture?

### What weakens it

Evidence that the complaint review went beyond the treating clinician's explanation, examined source materials and expressly addressed the provenance challenge.

### What defeats it

An independent complaint investigation that reconstructed the original phenotype, audited the creation and propagation of the disputed categories, tested contrary evidence and later boundary crossings, addressed PRKN genealogy, and explained why the resulting architecture remained defensible.

# Proposition 9 — Neuropathic terminology may have become more stable than the underlying disease proposition

### Raw evidence

The early formulation is expressly tentative: the painful foot material might be “completely separate,” perhaps secondary peripheral neuropathy. Later records use neuropathic terminology more routinely. By 2025 the complaint response refers to “peripheral neuropathic pain symptomatologies,” while disease-level neuropathy can still be expressed conditionally. The patient-authored material also notes that the terminology was progressively used by doctors, nurses and eventually the patient himself, while warning that circulation of a term is not necessarily independent validation.

By 2025 the record can simultaneously describe extreme foot pain, medication-related improvement and a still-conditional peripheral neuropathy formulation.

### Established fact

The language changes in grammatical confidence and function over time.

### Documentary inference

Uncertainty may have migrated from whether the phenotype was appropriately described as neuropathic to what caused an already-stabilised “neuropathic” symptom category.

### Serious proposition

**The neuropathic branch may show classification hardening without equivalent disease-level diagnostic hardening.**

### Strongest hospital answer

“Neuropathic pain” does not necessarily mean “proven peripheral neuropathy.”

Clinicians may be describing pain phenotype rather than disease mechanism.

The apparently stronger wording may therefore reflect semantic precision, not unjustified diagnostic hardening.

That is a particularly strong hospital answer.

### Status

**Plausible and partly supported.**

Indeed, the survival of conditional disease-level wording supports the possibility that clinicians were deliberately distinguishing pain phenotype from neuropathy as a disease.

### Hospital must explain

A source-by-source linguistic genealogy.

At each stage:

What exactly did “neuropathic” mean?

Was it a symptom descriptor?

A working mechanism?

A formal diagnosis?

A copied history?

What new evidence accompanied stronger language?

Were later authors independently assessing phenotype, or adopting the established term?

### What weakens it

Evidence that the record consistently uses “neuropathic” as phenotype language while explicitly preserving uncertainty about disease and cause.

### What defeats it

A clear source genealogy demonstrating that the semantic strengthening corresponds to new independent clinical evidence, and that later authors consciously distinguished symptom phenotype from disease mechanism rather than inheriting an increasingly authoritative label.

This proposition is therefore potentially vulnerable to careful record analysis. It should not be overstated.

From 2023 onward the record describes severe psychological and functional burden. Walking is described as psychologically consuming. Another walk becomes a “psychological mountain.” By 2024 life is “a lot smaller.” By 2025 the language includes “murder in the feet” and “torture.” The patient-authored formulation expressly separates this issue from the disputed pain mechanism and acknowledges that absence from the surviving record does not prove that absolutely nothing happened.

Severe persistent suffering and psychological burden were documented.

The severity of the human consequences can be assessed independently of whether the disputed foot mechanism was ultimately neurological, neuropathic, structural or mixed.

### Serious proposition

The serious proposition must be kept narrower than “the hospital did nothing.”

It is:

**The surviving record raises a question whether the escalating physical and psychological burden generated a sufficiently visible protective, risk-assessment or safeguarding response proportionate to the severity being documented.**

### Strongest hospital answer

There was response.

The patient continued to receive medical review.

The 2023 period produced genuine neurological and pain reconsideration.

Medication and treatment were adjusted.

Local intervention can itself be a legitimate response to distress.

Extreme language about suffering must not automatically be converted into proof of suicidal intention, a safeguarding emergency or a particular mandatory intervention.

### Status

**The broad accusation of no response is contradicted; the narrower safeguarding/documentation question remains unresolved and is likely expert/governance-dependent.**

This is important.

The hospital already possesses material capable of defeating an assertion that severe symptoms simply generated “no thought → same plan.”

The surviving formulation itself recognises genuine response.

### Hospital must explain

What risk assessment occurred?

What did clinicians understand the psychological language to signify?

Were self-harm or immediate safety concerns assessed where clinically indicated?

What protective advice, escalation, referral, safety planning or follow-up occurred?

Was the response recorded elsewhere?

Was ordinary clinical management reasonably sufficient in the circumstances?

### What weakens it

Evidence of active symptom reassessment, urgent review, follow-up and documented concern.

That evidence already exists to some degree.

### What defeats it

Contemporaneous documentation showing that the severity and psychological burden were recognised, appropriately assessed for relevant risk, responded to proportionately, safety-netted and followed up.

# Three different kinds of defeat

The case should not confuse documentary, clinical and legal defeat. The task expressly requires these levels to remain separate.

A **documentary defeat** occurs if missing contemporary material shows that the reconstruction is factually incomplete—for example, a detailed lower-limb examination, Complex Case Meeting note, explicit cross-specialty ownership plan, independent reassessment, PRKN closure record or independent complaint audit.

A **clinical defeat** can occur even if the documentary observation remains true. An expert might conclude that the 2017 examination, separation and later branch-level management were entirely reasonable clinical practice. That could defeat a breach allegation while leaving intact the documentary fact that the reasoning bridge is incompletely visible.

A **legal defeat** is further downstream again. Even surviving documentary and clinical criticism does not itself establish breach, factual causation, legal causation, injury or any other jurisdiction-specific element. The present source expressly disclaims a final negligence or legal conclusion.

# A. Propositions presently difficult for the hospital to answer documentarily

The first is the **founding evidential bridge**. The supplied architecture still does not identify at comparable resolution the positive and negative discriminators that transformed an integrated feet–gait–tone presentation into a durable Parkinsonian branch plus a substantially separate structural/peripheral branch. This does not establish that the clinical separation was wrong. It means the answer is not yet fully visible in the supplied documentary architecture.

The second is **residual ownership and reopening**. The record makes service destinations much easier to identify than responsibility for the unresolved relationship among services. Later reassessment proves that the system was permeable; it does not by itself identify the original governance rule for reconsideration.

The third is **global reconstruction**. The hospital already has substantial evidence of local re-analysis. What remains harder to identify is the event where the parent model was deliberately rebuilt from the source phenotype before the complaint-era challenge.

The fourth is the **PRKN closure bridge**. The hospital has a strong conceptual answer—syndrome is not etiology—but the presently supplied record still needs a clean explanation of how the live PRKN hypothesis related to later more settled terminology.

The fifth is the **complaint-review object**. The response is good evidence of historical rationale. The unresolved question is whether governance independently tested the architecture or principally tested the defensibility of management from inside it.

The sixth, separately, is the **protective-response record** once suffering became extreme. The record shows care and reassessment, so “no response” cannot fairly survive. What remains open is whether risk recognition and protective action were proportionately and visibly documented.

# B. Propositions for which the hospital already has substantial contrary evidence

The hospital has already substantially defeated any allegation that the lower-limb presentation was homogeneous. It plainly was not.

It has strong evidence that genuine structural pathology existed.

It has evidence that the patient himself distinguished certain symptom experiences.

It has strong evidence that Parkinsonism was correctly identified.

It has evidence that the dystonic-looking gait was recognised contemporaneously.

It has evidence that genetics and PARK2 were not simply ignored.

It has evidence of extensive specialist activity and external input.

It has powerful evidence against a static-framework theory in the 2020 and 2023 episodes.

It has evidence that new neuropathic hypotheses could arise from genuinely new clinical information rather than simply from copying 2017 terminology.

And it has evidence that severe later symptoms prompted real review and treatment rather than complete clinical inactivity.

Any future claimant formulation that ignores those facts will be easier to defeat than the present narrower information-architecture case.

# C. Serious propositions that could disappear if one missing category of evidence is produced

These are the highest-value disclosure targets.

**First: the complete 2017 examination and Complex Case Meeting material.** A record demonstrating that the integrated phenotype reached collective review, underwent appropriate distal neurological, structural, dynamic and pain discrimination, and was deliberately separated on reasoned grounds could collapse much of Propositions 1, 2 and the early-compression theory. The case formulation itself identifies contemporaneous meeting material showing that the integrated relationship was presented, debated and given a reasoned disposition or reopening plan as the strongest falsifier of the 24-hour compression hypothesis.

**Second: an explicit 2017 ownership/reopening plan.** One document saying, in effect, “mixed mechanisms remain possible; structural care proceeds; Neurology retains relationship ownership; return if X/Y/Z occurs” would radically weaken Proposition 3.

**Third: a pre-2025 global specialist reconstruction.** A note gathering structural disease, pain, dystonia, gait, medication state, OFF phenomena, neuropathic possibilities and genetic evidence into one renewed synthesis could materially defeat Proposition 5 and strongly weaken Propositions 4 and 7.

**Fourth: a PRKN closure or terminology document.** Negative testing, reasoned downgrading, or a clear explanation that later clinical terminology did not purport to resolve etiology could virtually eliminate Proposition 6.

**Fifth: the complete complaint-review file.** If it shows genuine independent source reconstruction, active testing of contrary evidence and independent specialist revalidation, Proposition 8 may disappear.

**Sixth: a contemporaneous risk/safeguarding response record.** If severe psychological and pain language produced appropriate assessment, escalation, safety-netting and follow-up, the narrow protective-response proposition could be defeated documentarily even though the severity of suffering would remain a fact.

# The hospital's real evidential task

The hospital does not need to establish that the patient was treated. Extensive treatment is already accepted.

It does not need to establish that Parkinsonism was real. That is accepted.

It does not need to prove structural foot disease. That is accepted.

It does not need to prove that later reconsideration occurred. That is accepted too.

Its difficult task is narrower:

**show what earned the founding separation; show how the unresolved relationship was owned and governed; distinguish later independent validation from inherited continuity; and explain why each major piece of boundary-crossing evidence did not require reconstruction at a broader level.** That is exactly the evidential explanation identified by the stress-test instructions.

If the hospital can establish those bridges contemporaneously, the central documentary case becomes much weaker.

If it cannot, demonstrating that each individual local decision was medically plausible does not necessarily answer the information-architecture proposition.

# After giving the hospital every fair defence, what survives?

The narrowest serious proposition that presently survives is this:

**A broad relational lower-limb and motor presentation appears to have been converted early into an authority-bearing divided architecture; the hospital has substantial evidence that the component phenomena were genuinely heterogeneous and that later clinicians repeatedly reconsidered individual questions, but the supplied record still demonstrates the management, local updating and documentary inheritance of the architecture more clearly than it demonstrates the evidential bridge that originally established the relevant branches as sufficiently independent, the explicit governance of the residual relationship, and repeated independent reconstruction of the parent model when later evidence crossed its boundaries.**

That is a documentary proposition.

It is **not** a negligence finding.

It is **not** a medical conclusion that the original separation was wrong.

It is **not** a finding that every painful foot symptom was dystonic or PRKN-mediated.

It is **not** a finding that the hospital ignored the patient.

It is **not** a finding of motive, concealment or bad faith.

The underlying formulation itself insists on those limits.

## What would defeat even that narrow proposition?

Put the strongest proposition and strongest falsifier side by side.

The proposition survives only while the relevant bridges remain absent from the supplied record.

It would be materially defeated by a coherent evidential chain showing all or most of the following:

1. the full relational GP phenotype was actually available to, understood by and used as the clinical object of the relevant specialist and collective decision-makers;
2. the 2017 lower-limb assessment contained adequate distal neurological, dynamic-posture, structural, loading and pain discrimination;
3. clinicians explicitly considered mixed and interacting mechanisms rather than converting coexistence into independence;
4. the original partition was reasoned and documented rather than merely made operational;
5. residual uncertainty had identified ownership, feedback routes and reopening triggers;
6. later clinicians independently revisited the disputed relationship rather than merely inheriting its terminology;
7. the major boundary-crossing events were expressly integrated and were reasonably found not to alter the founding model;
8. PRKN's unresolved status was properly tracked or later closed, and later terminology did not falsely imply etiological resolution;
9. recurrent GP reintegrations were received and answered at the appropriate specialist level;
10. the complaint process independently reconstructed the source architecture rather than relying principally on the coherence of the accumulated pathway.

If the hospital can produce that evidence, the information-architecture case may be largely or entirely documentarily defeated.

If it cannot, the surviving question is not “Were the doctors doing nothing?” The supplied record contradicts that.

It is more precise:

**Where is the evidential bridge that turns a clinically workable longitudinal model into a repeatedly independently validated one?**

That is presently the load-bearing question.

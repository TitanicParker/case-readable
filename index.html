Understand the case in order.

Start with the record. Then follow the same local story from compression, to challenge, to continuity, to the missing phenotype. The patient, GP and neurologist remain close throughout.

The basic line is simple: an integrated feet–gait–tone problem entered Neurology; Parkinsonism and dystonic-looking movement were recognised; the painful feet remained uncertain and partly separate; the patient objected before discharge; the same local clinicians remained; later treatment kept sending information back across that boundary; and 2026 finally identified PRKN/Parkin disease with dystonia.
